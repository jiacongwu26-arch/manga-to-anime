# Panel to shot grammar

## Core rule: a panel is not a shot

One page with 2–4 panels → 3–5 animation shots.

Manga carries time through multiple panels on one page; animation carries it through shot duration and editing. That translation is the whole job.

## Split decision table

| Panel content | Shots | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue, 2+ characters | 2–3 | Shot / reverse shot: A speaks → B reacts → back to A |
| Monologue panel | 1 | Speaking shot with slight camera move |
| Action panel | 1–2 | One continuous shot, or cut at action beats (start/mid/end) |
| Thought / borderless | 1 | Long static shot + depth of field + slow push |
| Large / bleed panel | 1 | Establishing shot or emotional beat |
| Inset panel | 1 (insert) | Insert close-up after the shot it explains |
| SFX panel | 0–1 | Never its own shot; layer the sound over the adjacent action shot |
| Narration | follows picture | Voice-over; does not own a shot |
| Flashback | 1 | Color treatment + hard cut in and out |

## Verified translation rules

1. **Panel ≠ shot, but they map**: a 2–4 panel page becomes 3–5 shots.
2. **Information density sets shot count**: more bubbles → more cuts; large/borderless panels → long takes.
3. **Medium enhancement**: depth of field, light direction, color emotion are things manga lacks and animation must add.
4. **Gutter time = edit rhythm**: hard cuts within an island, scene change across islands.
5. **Character consistency**: animation uses model sheets; the pipeline equivalent is character reference frames/images.

## Shot / reverse shot

```
Shot 1 (A speaks): medium, A facing B, all of A's lines
Shot 2 (B reacts): close, B's expression change, no lines, ~2.0s
(If B replies, add Shot 3: B speaks)
```

Reaction shots are the breathing room of dialogue; without them conversation feels mechanical.

## Shot budget per page

| Page type | Typical panels | Shots | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dialogue page | 3–4 | 4–6 | 10–16s |
| Action page | 2–3 | 3–4 | 8–12s |
| Monologue / mood | 1–2 | 1–3 | 6–10s |
| Establishing / scenery | 1 | 1–2 | 4–6s |

## Common errors

- One shot per page (turns into a motion comic)
- One shot per panel at a fixed 2s (mechanical rhythm)
- No reaction shot in dialogue scenes (characters look like they are reading lines)
- Treating an inset panel as its own narrative beat (breaks the logic)
